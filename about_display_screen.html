WHY SOME SCREENS FEEL MUCH HARSHER THAN OTHERS

A SIMPLE CAUSAL GUIDE TO PWM, FRC, PIXEL RESPONSE,
BRIGHTNESS, CONTRAST, SPECTRUM, SURFACE, AND THE “INK-LIKE” LOOK


============================================================
1. THE CENTRAL IDEA
============================================================

A screen does not simply “show an image.”

A display produces light, controls that light, changes pixel
values, processes colors, moves pixels through different states,
and sends the resulting optical signal through the screen surface
into your eyes.

That means two displays can show exactly the same webpage,
photograph, or text while producing a noticeably different
visual experience.

One screen may appear:

    solid
    clean
    stable
    natural
    calm
    ink-like

Another may appear:

    glowing
    overly bright
    grainy
    sparkly
    hazy
    washed out
    unstable
    electrically intense

There is no single specification that explains this.

Not:

    IPS
    VA
    TN
    OLED
    8-bit
    8-bit + FRC
    “flicker-free”
    “low blue light”
    1 ms
    5 ms
    high refresh rate
    high contrast

The useful question is:

    WHAT IS THIS PARTICULAR DISPLAY ACTUALLY DOING?

The goal is to separate the different physical mechanisms
instead of blaming one technology label for everything.


============================================================
2. THE COMPLETE CAUSAL MODEL
============================================================

A useful simplified chain is:

    DISPLAY HARDWARE
          ↓
    LIGHT SOURCE / OLED EMISSION
          ↓
    SPECTRAL DISTRIBUTION
          ↓
    LUMINANCE / BRIGHTNESS
          ↓
    BRIGHTNESS CONTROL
          ↓
    PWM OR DC DIMMING
          ↓
    COLOR PROCESSING
          ↓
    DITHERING / FRC
          ↓
    PIXEL RESPONSE
          ↓
    PIXEL / SUBPIXEL STRUCTURE
          ↓
    GAMMA / TONAL DISTRIBUTION
          ↓
    BLACK LEVEL + WHITE LEVEL + CONTRAST
          ↓
    SCREEN SURFACE
          ↓
    REFLECTION + SCATTERING + AG SPARKLE
          ↓
    FINAL OPTICAL IMAGE
          ↓
    LIGHT ENTERING THE EYE
          ↓
    RETINAL SIGNAL
          ↓
    VISUAL PROCESSING
          ↓
    FOCUS / TRACKING / BLINKING / ATTENTION
          ↓
    VISUAL WORKLOAD
          ↓
    PERCEIVED IMAGE CHARACTER
          ↓
    POSSIBLE DISCOMFORT


This chain contains several different problems.

They should not be treated as one thing.


============================================================
3. FOUR MAJOR DIMENSIONS OF DISPLAY BEHAVIOR
============================================================

The easiest way to understand the whole subject is to divide it
into four broad dimensions.

A. TEMPORAL BEHAVIOR

    What changes over time?

    PWM
    FRC
    temporal dithering
    pixel transitions
    motion behavior


B. SPATIAL BEHAVIOR

    What changes across the physical image?

    pixel structure
    subpixels
    gamma
    edges
    contrast
    surface texture
    AG sparkle
    scattering


C. OPTICAL INTENSITY

    How strong is the optical stimulus?

    brightness
    white luminance
    contrast
    spectral distribution


D. FINAL PERCEPTION

    What does all of this look like together?

    solid
    clean
    stable
    natural
    ink-like

    OR:

    glowing
    grainy
    sparkly
    hazy
    unstable
    harsh
    electronic


This separation is important because a display can be good in one
dimension and problematic in another.


============================================================
4. THE SCREEN IS AN EMISSIVE OBJECT
============================================================

Printed paper is mainly reflective.

You normally have:

    room light
         ↓
    paper
         ↓
    reflected light
         ↓
    eye

A monitor produces its own light:

    electrical signal
         ↓
    display
         ↓
    emitted light
         ↓
    eye

Therefore a white webpage on a monitor is not physically
equivalent to a white sheet of paper.

The monitor is producing light.

This is one reason brightness matters so much.

A display can technically reproduce white correctly and still
look very different from another display because its luminance,
spectrum, surface, and tonal behavior are different.


============================================================
5. PWM: BRIGHTNESS CAN CHANGE OVER TIME
============================================================

PWM means:

    Pulse Width Modulation.

A display using PWM can control brightness by rapidly changing
the light source between ON and OFF states.

Conceptually:

    ON  ON  ON  OFF  OFF
    ON  ON  ON  OFF  OFF
    ON  ON  ON  OFF  OFF

The average light output can appear to be one steady brightness
to the eye.

But physically the light output is changing over time.

This is different from continuously reducing the output.


============================================================
6. DC DIMMING
============================================================

DC dimming reduces the light output more continuously.

Conceptually:

    HIGH OUTPUT
        ↓
    MEDIUM OUTPUT
        ↓
    LOW OUTPUT

rather than:

    FULL
    OFF
    FULL
    OFF
    FULL
    OFF

This is why PWM and DC dimming are different mechanisms.

However:

    DC DIMMING ≠ AUTOMATICALLY PERFECT

A display using DC dimming can still have:

    FRC
    temporal dithering
    surface sparkle
    excessive brightness
    strong contrast
    poor tonal behavior
    unsuitable spectral characteristics

Therefore brightness modulation is only one part of the problem.


============================================================
7. “FLICKER-FREE” HAS A LIMITED MEANING
============================================================

“Flicker-free” generally refers to reducing or avoiding detectable
or problematic brightness modulation, usually associated with PWM.

It does NOT automatically mean:

    no FRC
    no temporal dithering
    no pixel instability
    no surface sparkle
    no haze
    no excessive brightness
    no harsh contrast
    no spectral issues
    no visual workload

Therefore:

    FLICKER-FREE
    ≠
    VISUALLY PERFECT


============================================================
8. PWM AND FRC ARE NOT THE SAME
============================================================

This distinction is fundamental.

PWM primarily concerns:

    LIGHT OUTPUT CHANGING OVER TIME.

FRC primarily concerns:

    PIXEL VALUES CHANGING OVER TIME
    TO SIMULATE INTERMEDIATE COLORS.

They can occur independently.

A display can be:

    PWM-free + FRC-active

or:

    PWM-active + FRC-free

or:

    both

or:

    neither in a noticeable form.


============================================================
9. FRC: SIMULATING COLOR LEVELS
============================================================

FRC means:

    Frame Rate Control.

Suppose a panel cannot directly produce the exact shade that
the image requests.

It may alternate between nearby shades:

    A
    B
    A
    B
    A
    B

The visual system integrates the rapid sequence and can perceive
an intermediate result.

This is a form of temporal dithering.

The important point is:

    THE IMAGE CAN LOOK STATIC WHILE THE PIXEL VALUES
    ARE CHANGING OVER TIME.


============================================================
10. WHY FRC CAN BE DIFFICULT TO SEE
============================================================

FRC does not necessarily look like obvious flashing.

Depending on the implementation, it may appear as:

    subtle shimmer
    crawling texture
    fine grain
    unstable dark tones
    tiny moving noise
    faint vibrating texture

Some people may not consciously notice it.

The effect can also depend on:

    color
    brightness
    refresh rate
    pixel response
    viewing distance
    image content
    individual sensitivity

Therefore a casual glance is not enough to establish temporal
behavior.


============================================================
11. GRADIENT SMOOTHNESS AND TEMPORAL STABILITY ARE DIFFERENT
============================================================

This is one of the most important testing distinctions.

A smooth gradient means:

    the visible transition between colors is smooth.

Temporal stability means:

    an apparently fixed color remains physically or perceptually
    stable rather than alternating over time.

These are not the same.

A display can have:

    beautiful smooth gradients
    + temporal dithering

Another can have:

    slight banding
    + very stable pixel output

Therefore:

    SMOOTHNESS ≠ STABILITY

and:

    BANDING ≠ AUTOMATICALLY BAD.


============================================================
12. NATIVE 8-BIT VS 8-BIT + FRC
============================================================

8-bit per channel provides:

    256 levels per color channel.

An 8-bit + FRC implementation can use temporal alternation to
simulate additional levels.

This means:

    native color depth

and:

    temporal behavior

are related but not identical questions.

A panel described as “8-bit” does not automatically prove that
every stage of the display pipeline is temporally static.

Likewise, “8-bit + FRC” does not automatically tell you how
noticeable its temporal behavior will be.


============================================================
13. DITHERING CAN HAPPEN AT DIFFERENT STAGES
============================================================

Dithering may potentially be introduced at different points
in the signal path:

    application
        ↓
    operating system / compositor
        ↓
    GPU
        ↓
    display interface
        ↓
    monitor electronics / TCON
        ↓
    panel

But this does NOT mean every modern device necessarily dithers
at every stage.

The actual implementation depends on the hardware and software.

Therefore statements such as:

    “the OS always dithers”

or:

    “the GPU always dithers”

or:

    “the monitor always performs FRC”

are too broad without evidence.


============================================================
14. GPU REGISTRY / DITHER SETTINGS
============================================================

Some GPU drivers or configurations expose values associated with
dithering.

These can be useful as:

    diagnostic clues
    experimental variables

For example, changing a driver-level dithering setting and
observing whether a controlled test image changes can provide
useful evidence.

But such a registry value is NOT automatically proof that:

    FRC is occurring on the monitor.

It is important to distinguish:

    “this setting changes something”

from:

    “this proves the exact physical mechanism.”


============================================================
15. PIXEL RESPONSE TIME
============================================================

Pixel response time describes how quickly a pixel changes from
one state to another.

A slow transition can produce:

    ghosting
    trailing
    motion blur
    dark smearing

A faster transition can produce:

    cleaner motion
    less trailing
    sharper transitions

But:

    FAST ≠ AUTOMATICALLY MORE COMFORTABLE

and:

    SLOW ≠ AUTOMATICALLY MORE COMFORTABLE

Pixel response and FRC are separate mechanisms.


============================================================
16. SLOW PIXELS DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY REMOVE FRC
============================================================

It is tempting to think:

    “If the pixel is slow, it will average out FRC.”

That is not a safe universal assumption.

Pixel response determines how the physical pixel transitions.

FRC determines how requested values may alternate.

The two mechanisms interact, but one does not automatically
cancel the other.

A slow panel may make temporal changes appear differently.

It does not automatically make the display temporally static.


============================================================
17. VERY SLOW RESPONSE AND MOTION
============================================================

Consider black text moving over a white background.

A slow panel may produce:

    black
       ↓
    gray transition
       ↓
    white

The result can be a visible trail.

A faster panel can produce a more distinct transition:

    black
       ↓
    white

For normal display quality, the target is not simply:

    “slow”

or:

    “fast.”

The target is:

    CLEAN AND CONTROLLED TRANSITIONS.


============================================================
18. BRIGHTNESS
============================================================

Brightness determines how much light the display sends toward
your eyes.

A very bright white background can produce a strong optical
stimulus.

For example:

    moderate white
    → controlled

versus:

    extremely bright white
    → much stronger illumination

This can affect the visual character of the entire screen.

Excessive brightness can make a display appear:

    glowing
    shiny
    aggressive
    electronic

Controlled brightness can make the same content appear:

    calmer
    more solid
    more natural
    more ink-like

Brightness is therefore both:

    a measurable optical property

and:

    a major perceptual variable.


============================================================
19. BRIGHTNESS IS NOT THE SAME AS IMAGE QUALITY
============================================================

A screen being brighter does not automatically mean it is better.

Modern displays may be designed to produce:

    very high brightness
    HDR highlights
    strong white output

Those are useful capabilities.

But more optical output is not automatically preferable for every
person or every task.

The practical target is:

    ENOUGH BRIGHTNESS TO SEE THE IMAGE CLEARLY

without unnecessarily turning the entire display into a strong
light source.


============================================================
20. CONTRAST
============================================================

Contrast describes the difference between dark and bright parts
of the image.

High contrast can make text and objects very distinct.

For example:

    black text
    on
    white background

has strong local contrast.

That can improve readability.

However, very strong luminance differences can also make an image
feel visually intense.

This does NOT mean:

    high contrast = harmful

It means:

    contrast is part of the optical signal being processed.


============================================================
21. LOCAL CONTRAST AND EDGE PROCESSING
============================================================

The visual system is particularly sensitive to edges.

An edge can be:

    BLACK | WHITE

or:

    DARK GRAY | SLIGHTLY LIGHTER GRAY

The visual system emphasizes boundaries because they carry
important information about objects.

This is part of normal visual processing.

Phenomena such as Mach bands demonstrate that perceived
brightness near an edge can differ from the simple physical
luminance values.

The important practical point is:

    STRONG LOCAL CONTRAST CAN MAKE EDGES FEEL MORE INTENSE.

This does not mean that high contrast is inherently harmful.

It means that contrast contributes to the final perceptual
character of the screen.


============================================================
22. GAMMA
============================================================

Gamma affects how digital signal values are mapped to displayed
luminance.

Two monitors can have similar:

    black level
    white level
    contrast ratio

while still distributing intermediate tones differently.

That can substantially change the appearance of an image.

If dark tones are lifted too much:

    black becomes gray
    dark detail becomes hazy
    the image can look washed out

If tonal transitions are well controlled:

    dark objects look solid
    intermediate tones remain distinct
    the image can look more natural.


============================================================
23. THE “WHITE VEIL” EFFECT
============================================================

A display can have blacks that are technically visible but still
look cloudy.

Instead of:

    solid black

you may perceive:

    black + gray haze

or:

    black + white veil

This reduces the feeling that objects are solid.

The image can therefore appear:

    washed
    cloudy
    hazy

rather than:

    dense
    solid
    ink-like

This is one reason numerical contrast ratio alone does not tell
the whole visual story.


============================================================
24. BLACK LEVEL AND WHITE LEVEL MUST BE CONSIDERED TOGETHER
============================================================

Imagine:

    Display A
        good black
        controlled white

and:

    Display B
        grayish black
        very bright white

Even if both display the same content, their visual character
can be very different.

Display B may appear more:

    glowing
    washed
    electronically lit

The relationship between:

    black
    gray
    white

is important for perceived solidity.


============================================================
25. THE SPECTRUM OF THE LIGHT
============================================================

Not all display light has the same spectral composition.

Different technologies can use different light sources and
different mixtures of wavelengths.

For example, many LCD monitors use white LEDs whose spectral
distribution differs from older CCFL systems.

This can change the character of the emitted light.

Shorter wavelengths also scatter more strongly in the eye than
longer wavelengths.

However, this should NOT be simplified into:

    “blue light automatically causes eye damage”

or:

    “blue light automatically causes ciliary muscle spasms.”

Those claims are much stronger than the basic optical evidence.

Spectrum should instead be treated as:

    ANOTHER VARIABLE THAT CAN CHANGE THE VISUAL CHARACTER
    OF THE DISPLAY.


============================================================
26. CHROMATIC ABERRATION
============================================================

The eye's optical system does not focus every wavelength at
exactly the same location.

This is called:

    chromatic aberration.

Different wavelengths can therefore have slightly different
optical behavior.

Shorter wavelengths also tend to scatter more strongly.

These are real optical properties.

But they should not automatically be converted into a specific
medical explanation for every person's discomfort.

The useful engineering approach is:

    identify the spectrum
    ↓
    observe the actual display
    ↓
    compare displays
    ↓
    treat spectrum as one variable among many.


============================================================
27. COLOR TEMPERATURE IS NOT THE WHOLE SPECTRUM
============================================================

A monitor may provide settings such as:

    6500 K
    7500 K
    9300 K

These change the overall color balance.

But color temperature is not a complete description of the
physical spectral distribution.

Two displays can both be:

    6500 K

and still produce different spectral distributions.

Therefore:

    COLOR TEMPERATURE
    ≠
    COMPLETE SPECTRAL DESCRIPTION.


============================================================
28. LOW-BLUE-LIGHT MODES
============================================================

Reducing short-wavelength output changes the spectral balance.

That may change how the screen looks.

But a display can have a low-blue-light mode and still have:

    PWM
    FRC
    temporal dithering
    excessive brightness
    AG sparkle
    poor gamma
    washed blacks
    excessive contrast

Therefore:

    LOW BLUE LIGHT
    ≠
    AUTOMATICALLY COMFORTABLE

It is one variable, not a complete solution.


============================================================
29. HARDWARE LOW-BLUE-LIGHT VS SOFTWARE COLOR FILTERS
============================================================

A software color filter changes the image signal.

A hardware-level spectral modification can change the actual
light spectrum emitted by the display.

These are not identical.

Therefore a warm-looking image does not necessarily prove that
the physical spectrum has been reduced in the same way as a
hardware low-blue-light implementation.

Again:

    WHAT THE IMAGE LOOKS LIKE

and:

    WHAT THE DISPLAY PHYSICALLY EMITS

are related but not identical questions.


============================================================
30. ANTI-GLARE SURFACES
============================================================

The screen surface affects the image after the pixels have
already produced it.

Anti-glare coatings scatter incoming light.

Their purpose is to reduce reflections.

This is useful in bright environments.

However, some aggressive matte coatings can introduce visible
surface texture.

That can appear as:

    grain
    sparkle
    crystalline texture
    haze

This is commonly described as:

    AG SPARKLE

where AG means:

    anti-glare.


============================================================
31. AG SPARKLE
============================================================

Imagine a perfectly clean white area.

On a smooth surface it may appear:

    uniform white

On a strongly textured matte surface it may appear:

    white
    + tiny variations
    + grain
    + sparkle

The actual pixel values may be identical.

The physical surface is changing how the light is scattered.

This can reduce perceived image cleanliness.

Therefore:

    PIXEL QUALITY
    +
    SURFACE QUALITY

both matter.


============================================================
32. MATTE VS GLOSSY
============================================================

Matte surfaces:

    reduce reflections
    work well in bright environments

but can introduce:

    grain
    sparkle
    scattering
    haze

Glossy or semi-gloss surfaces:

    can preserve a cleaner-looking image
    can produce stronger reflections

Neither is universally superior.

The environment matters.

A glossy screen with a bright reflection may be worse than a
matte screen.

A heavily textured matte screen can look less clean than a
smooth surface.

The correct question is:

    HOW MUCH REFLECTION CONTROL IS NEEDED
    WITHOUT DESTROYING IMAGE CLEANLINESS?


============================================================
33. WHY SURFACE TEXTURE MATTERS TO TEXT
============================================================

Consider a black letter:

    █

A clean surface can make it look:

    solid
    uniform
    sharply defined

A heavily textured surface can make it look:

    sharp
    but grainy

The letter is still technically sharp.

Its visual surface is simply less uniform.

This distinction becomes important for the “ink-like” quality.


============================================================
34. IPS, VA, AND TN
============================================================

Panel type changes the physical behavior of the display.

IPS commonly offers:

    good viewing angles
    relatively consistent color across the screen

VA commonly offers:

    high native contrast
    deeper blacks

but can have:

    slower dark transitions
    dark smearing on some implementations

TN commonly offers:

    fast transitions
    different viewing-angle behavior
    different contrast/color characteristics

These are general tendencies.

They are NOT rules about visual comfort.

There is no valid universal equation:

    IPS = bad
    VA = good
    TN = good

or the reverse.

The actual implementation matters more than the label.


============================================================
35. VIEWING ANGLE AND GAMMA BEHAVIOR
============================================================

Different panel technologies can change appearance when viewed
off-axis.

TN panels can show stronger viewing-angle changes.

This can cause:

    brightness shifts
    gamma shifts
    color changes

across the screen when your viewing angle changes.

This is a display characteristic, not automatically an eye-health
problem.

It can nevertheless affect visual consistency and perceived
image quality.


============================================================
36. PIXEL / SUBPIXEL STRUCTURE
============================================================

The final image is built from physical pixels and subpixels.

Their structure affects:

    text edges
    fine detail
    color boundaries
    apparent sharpness

The same resolution does not guarantee the same visual result.

Two panels can both be:

    1920 × 1080

while producing different text appearance because of differences
in:

    subpixel arrangement
    pixel geometry
    rendering
    coating
    scaling
    gamma


============================================================
37. SHARPNESS IS NOT THE SAME AS HARSHNESS
============================================================

This distinction is essential.

A display can be:

    SHARP + INK-LIKE

Sharpness means:

    clean, well-defined edges.

Harshness can involve:

    excessive brightness
    strong luminance
    sparkle
    unstable pixels
    aggressive contrast
    glowing whites
    distracting grain

Therefore:

    SHARP ≠ HARSH

and:

    SOFT ≠ COMFORTABLE

The useful target is:

    SHARP
    +
    SOLID
    +
    STABLE
    +
    CONTROLLED


============================================================
38. WHAT “INK-LIKE” MEANS
============================================================

“INK-LIKE” is not a standard technical specification.

There is no universal measurement called:

    ink-likeness.

It is a description of the final visual appearance.

An ink-like image tends to look:

    solid
    clean
    stable
    quiet
    controlled
    naturally integrated with the screen surface

Text looks like a solid shape.

Dark objects look dense rather than veiled.

White areas look controlled rather than like a powerful light
source.

Edges are clean rather than glowing or sparkling.

The image does not appear to contain unnecessary visual noise.


============================================================
39. INK-LIKE DOES NOT MEAN LOW CONTRAST
============================================================

This is another important distinction.

“Ink-like” does NOT mean:

    low contrast
    blurry
    dull
    dim
    soft

Printed ink can have strong contrast.

The target is:

    CLEAN CONTRAST
    without
    EXCESSIVE LUMINANCE INTENSITY.


============================================================
40. INK-LIKE DOES NOT MEAN LOW SHARPNESS
============================================================

A good ink-like display can have extremely sharp text.

The difference is that the letter looks:

    solid
    stable
    clean

rather than:

    glowing
    sparkling
    crawling
    blooming
    electrically intense

So the desirable combination is:

    SHARP
        +
    STABLE
        +
    CLEAN
        +
    MODERATE LUMINANCE


============================================================
41. WHAT CONTRIBUTES TO THE INK-LIKE LOOK?
============================================================

There is no single cause.

It can result from the combination of:

    controlled brightness
    controlled white luminance
    good black level
    controlled gamma
    clean tonal transitions
    appropriate contrast
    stable pixel output
    low unwanted temporal modulation
    suitable pixel response
    clean pixel/subpixel structure
    low visible surface sparkle
    suitable surface
    suitable spectral character


The important concept is:

    INK-LIKE IS AN EMERGENT PERCEPTUAL RESULT.

It is produced by many technical variables interacting.


============================================================
42. BRIGHTNESS AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

If white is excessively bright:

    the page can feel emissive.

If white is controlled:

    the page can feel more like a stable visual surface.

Therefore:

    TOO BRIGHT
    → glowing / electronic

    CONTROLLED
    → solid / natural

This does not mean that an ink-like display must be dim.

It means that its luminance should be appropriate to the
environment and viewing task.


============================================================
43. CONTRAST AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

Very strong black-white contrast can make edges extremely
prominent.

But reducing contrast too far can make the image washed out.

Therefore the target is not:

    minimum contrast.

It is:

    CONTROLLED CONTRAST

where:

    black remains solid
    white remains controlled
    intermediate tones remain distinct
    edges remain clear


============================================================
44. GAMMA AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

Good tonal distribution helps objects appear solid.

Poorly controlled dark tones can create:

    gray blacks
    cloudy shadows
    white veil
    washed appearance

The result feels less like ink.

A useful simplified model is:

    CONTROLLED TONES
        ↓
    SOLID OBJECTS
        ↓
    INK-LIKE

while:

    WASHED TONES
        ↓
    VEILED OBJECTS
        ↓
    LESS INK-LIKE


============================================================
45. SURFACE AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

A clean image can be damaged perceptually by a dirty-looking
surface.

Aggressive matte texture can introduce:

    sparkle
    grain
    haze

This can make text feel less like ink even when the panel itself
is sharp.

A smoother surface can preserve:

    clean edges
    uniform fills
    clearer fine detail

but may introduce reflections.

Therefore surface quality is part of the ink-like equation.


============================================================
46. TEMPORAL STABILITY AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

An ink-like letter should look like:

    one stable object.

If the pixel values subtly alternate, the letter or surrounding
area may appear to contain:

    shimmer
    crawling grain
    unstable texture

This can make the image feel less solid.

Therefore:

    TEMPORAL STABILITY
    contributes to
    PERCEIVED SOLIDITY.


============================================================
47. PIXEL RESPONSE AND THE INK-LIKE LOOK
============================================================

Very slow response can produce:

    trailing
    ghosting
    smearing

That makes moving objects less solid.

Extremely fast response is not automatically a problem, but speed
alone is not the goal.

The useful target is:

    CLEAN MOTION
    +
    STABLE STATIC IMAGE.


============================================================
48. THE TWO TYPES OF CLEANLINESS
============================================================

A useful combined model is:

    TEMPORAL CLEANLINESS

    +
    
    SPATIAL CLEANLINESS


TEMPORAL CLEANLINESS:

    stable light output
    stable pixel values
    low unwanted PWM
    low unwanted temporal dithering
    controlled pixel transitions


SPATIAL CLEANLINESS:

    clean pixel structure
    clean subpixel rendering
    controlled gamma
    good tonal transitions
    controlled contrast
    low distracting surface texture
    low AG sparkle


Together:

    TEMPORAL CLEANLINESS
          +
    SPATIAL CLEANLINESS
          ↓
    CLEAN IMAGE


============================================================
49. ADD OPTICAL INTENSITY
============================================================

Now add:

    brightness
    white luminance
    contrast
    spectrum

These determine the strength and character of the optical signal.

Therefore:

    TEMPORAL CLEANLINESS
          +
    SPATIAL CLEANLINESS
          +
    OPTICAL CONTROL
          ↓
    SOLID + CLEAN + STABLE IMAGE
          ↓
    INK-LIKE CHARACTER


============================================================
50. WHY A TECHNICALLY GOOD MONITOR CAN STILL LOOK BAD
============================================================

Imagine a monitor with:

    high refresh rate
    very fast response
    high brightness
    wide color gamut
    high contrast

These are impressive specifications.

But suppose it also has:

    extremely bright white
    aggressive matte sparkle
    washed dark tones
    strong local contrast
    visible temporal instability

The specification sheet can look excellent.

The actual image can still look:

    harsh
    glowing
    grainy
    electronic


This demonstrates:

    SPECIFICATION QUALITY
    ≠
    PERCEPTUAL QUALITY.


============================================================
51. WHY AN OLDER SCREEN CAN SOMETIMES LOOK MORE NATURAL
============================================================

Older displays may have combinations such as:

    CCFL backlighting
    different coatings
    lower peak brightness
    different gamma
    different contrast
    slower response
    different color characteristics

A newer display may be technically more advanced while having a
different visual character.

This does NOT establish:

    OLD = BETTER

or:

    NEW = WORSE

It means that the combination of physical characteristics has
changed.

The important question remains:

    WHICH CHARACTERISTICS PRODUCE THE APPEARANCE YOU ACTUALLY
    PREFER?


============================================================
52. WHY “MORE ADVANCED” DOES NOT MEAN “NICER TO LOOK AT”
============================================================

Modern displays are often optimized for measurable performance:

    high refresh rate
    high brightness
    HDR
    wide gamut
    high contrast
    very fast response
    thin design

Those are useful engineering goals.

But the preferred visual character may be different.

For example:

    500-nit white

is objectively more capable than:

    250-nit white

in terms of maximum luminance.

But that does not mean the 500-nit display will look better
for every person or every task.

Likewise:

    1 ms

does not automatically look better than:

    8 ms

for every visual situation.


============================================================
53. WHAT CAN ACTUALLY BE INFERRED FROM TESTS?
============================================================

Different tests answer different questions.

PWM test:

    “Is the light output changing over time?”

FRC / temporal test:

    “Are apparently static colors showing temporal behavior?”

Response-time test:

    “How quickly do pixels transition?”

Gradient test:

    “How smoothly are tones represented?”

But:

    smooth gradient
    ≠
    temporal stability

Surface inspection:

    “Does the coating produce visible grain or sparkle?”

Black-level test:

    “Do dark areas remain solid or look gray/veiled?”

White test:

    “Does white look controlled or excessively luminous?”

Text test:

    “Do letters look solid and clean?”


No single test answers all of these.


============================================================
54. TEST PWM SEPARATELY
============================================================

First determine whether brightness modulation is present.

Do not combine this with an FRC conclusion.

If PWM is absent, that means:

    PWM is probably not the main temporal mechanism being
    observed.

It does NOT prove:

    no temporal modulation of any kind.


============================================================
55. TEST FRC SEPARATELY
============================================================

Use controlled gradients and static color fields.

Pay attention to:

    dark gray
    near-black
    subtle gradients
    intermediate colors

Look for:

    shimmer
    crawling
    moving grain
    unstable texture

The important question is:

    DOES A SUPPOSEDLY STATIC COLOR REMAIN VISUALLY STABLE?


============================================================
56. WHY DARK COLORS ARE USEFUL FOR TEMPORAL TESTING
============================================================

Near-black and dark gray areas can be useful because small
changes in luminance can become noticeable as texture.

Use:

    dark gray gradients
    near-black gradients
    dark neutral colors

Then compare displays under the same conditions.

Do not ask only:

    “Which gradient is smoother?”

Ask:

    “Which gradient is more temporally stable?”


============================================================
57. TEST BRIGHT WHITE
============================================================

Open a mostly white webpage.

Compare:

    brightness
    surface
    white luminance
    apparent glow

Ask:

    Does the white look like a clean visual surface?

or:

    Does it look like a powerful light source?


============================================================
58. TEST BLACK
============================================================

Open a dark image.

Look at:

    black
    near-black
    dark gray
    medium gray

Ask:

    Are dark areas solid?

or:

    Are they gray, cloudy, or veiled?


============================================================
59. TEST TEXT
============================================================

Text combines many display properties simultaneously.

It reveals:

    pixel structure
    subpixel behavior
    gamma
    brightness
    local contrast
    surface texture
    temporal stability
    scaling

Look at:

    black text on white
    gray text on white
    white text on dark gray
    small text
    medium text

Ask:

    Does the letter look solid?

or:

    Does it glow, sparkle, crawl, shimmer, or look hazy?


============================================================
60. TEST LARGE COLOR AREAS
============================================================

Use large fields of:

    red
    green
    blue
    gray
    dark gray
    near-black

Do not judge only color accuracy.

Look for:

    grain
    shimmer
    brightness
    instability
    surface texture
    excessive intensity

A color can be technically accurate while still looking visually
unpleasant.


============================================================
61. TEST MOTION SEPARATELY
============================================================

Moving objects reveal:

    pixel response
    ghosting
    smearing
    temporal artifacts

Use moving text or controlled motion.

Compare:

    trailing
    blur
    overshoot
    dark smearing

Do not use motion performance as a direct measure of static-image
comfort.


============================================================
62. ONE VARIABLE AT A TIME
============================================================

This is one of the most important practical rules.

Do not simultaneously change:

    brightness
    contrast
    color temperature
    scaling
    refresh rate
    GPU settings

and then conclude:

    “This fixed the problem.”

Instead:

    CHANGE ONE VARIABLE
        ↓
    OBSERVE
        ↓
    RESTORE
        ↓
    CHANGE THE NEXT VARIABLE


This allows you to identify causality instead of correlation.


============================================================
63. PERSONAL SENSITIVITY VARIES
============================================================

Different people can respond differently to the same display.

One person may barely notice:

    PWM

while another notices it immediately.

One person may ignore:

    AG sparkle

while another finds the texture obvious.

One person may prefer:

    high contrast

while another prefers:

    softer tonal rendering.

Therefore personal testing is useful.

But personal observation should be used to identify:

    “This particular display behaves badly for me”

rather than to conclude:

    “This technology is universally harmful.”


============================================================
64. DO NOT CONFUSE CORRELATION WITH CAUSATION
============================================================

If changing a monitor makes discomfort disappear, that tells you:

    something about that display differs in a way that matters
    to you.

It does not automatically prove:

    PWM caused it.

It could involve:

    brightness
    spectrum
    surface
    FRC
    gamma
    contrast
    pixel response
    viewing angle
    text rendering
    multiple factors together.


The strongest approach is:

    OBSERVE
        ↓
    ISOLATE
        ↓
    TEST
        ↓
    COMPARE


============================================================
65. IMPORTANT CLAIMS THAT SHOULD NOT BE OVERSTATED
============================================================

Some explanations are often presented too strongly.

For example, it is not appropriate to automatically claim:

    “the retina processes invisible flicker up to exactly 200 Hz
     in every relevant situation”

or:

    “the pupils rapidly dilate and constrict hundreds of times
     per second because of PWM”

or:

    “FRC directly causes ciliary muscle spasms”

or:

    “the magnocellular pathway is being triggered nonstop”

or:

    “trigeminal dysphoria is the established mechanism of
     screen-related pain.”

These are much stronger claims than the basic display and optical
facts establish.

The safer engineering model is:

    TEMPORAL MODULATION
        ↓
    DIFFERENT OPTICAL SIGNAL
        ↓
    DIFFERENT VISUAL PROCESSING DEMAND
        ↓
    SOME PEOPLE MAY PERCEIVE IT DIFFERENTLY


This keeps the physical mechanism separate from speculation
about a specific medical mechanism.


============================================================
66. WHY “3840 Hz PWM” IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY SAFE
============================================================

A high PWM frequency can reduce the visibility of modulation.

But:

    HIGH PWM FREQUENCY

does not automatically mean:

    PERFECTLY COMFORTABLE DISPLAY.

The display may still have:

    FRC
    temporal dithering
    excessive brightness
    surface sparkle
    spectral differences
    strong contrast
    poor tonal behavior

Therefore the number alone should not be treated as a universal
comfort guarantee.


============================================================
67. WHY A COLOR MODE DOES NOT NECESSARILY “STOP DITHERING”
============================================================

Changing to:

    sRGB
    Natural
    Reading
    Eye Saver
    Warm

can change image processing.

But you should not automatically assume that a particular mode:

    completely disables temporal dithering.

A mode may change:

    gamut
    brightness
    gamma
    color balance

without changing every stage of temporal processing.

Again:

    OBSERVED CHANGE
    ≠
    PROOF OF THE EXACT MECHANISM.


============================================================
68. WHY PANEL RESPONSE IS NOT A UNIVERSAL “LOW-PASS FILTER”
============================================================

It is tempting to say that a very slow LCD will automatically
average away temporal modulation.

Real displays are more complicated.

The panel response has:

    transition-dependent behavior
    different behavior for different colors
    different behavior for different directions of transition

Therefore a slow response can change the appearance of temporal
modulation, but it should not be treated as a guaranteed physical
filter that eliminates FRC.


============================================================
69. THE DISPLAY PIPELINE IS THE IMPORTANT OBJECT
============================================================

Do not think of:

    “the monitor”

as a single simple component.

Think:

    SOURCE
      ↓
    APPLICATION
      ↓
    OPERATING SYSTEM
      ↓
    GPU
      ↓
    DISPLAY INTERFACE
      ↓
    MONITOR ELECTRONICS
      ↓
    TCON
      ↓
    PANEL
      ↓
    SURFACE
      ↓
    EYE


Different forms of processing can occur at different stages.

Therefore when troubleshooting temporal behavior, you are really
trying to determine:

    WHERE IS THE CHANGE BEING INTRODUCED?


============================================================
70. THE INK-LIKE TARGET
============================================================

The practical visual target can be summarized as:

    STABLE LIGHT
        +
    CONTROLLED BRIGHTNESS
        +
    CONTROLLED WHITE
        +
    SOLID BLACK
        +
    CLEAN TONAL TRANSITIONS
        +
    CONTROLLED CONTRAST
        +
    LOW UNWANTED TEMPORAL MODULATION
        +
    ADEQUATE PIXEL RESPONSE
        +
    CLEAN PIXEL / SUBPIXEL STRUCTURE
        +
    LOW DISTRACTING SURFACE SPARKLE
        +
    SUITABLE SPECTRUM
        ↓
    SOLID + CLEAN + STABLE IMAGE
        ↓
    INK-LIKE


This is a perceptual target, not a technical specification.


============================================================
71. INK-LIKE VS ELECTRONIC-LOOKING
============================================================

INK-LIKE:

    solid
    stable
    clean
    controlled
    natural
    dense darks
    controlled whites
    clean edges
    uniform surfaces
    little distracting grain
    little distracting shimmer


ELECTRONIC / HARSH:

    glowing
    excessively bright
    sparkly
    grainy
    hazy
    unstable
    washed
    veiled
    overly intense
    visually noisy


These are descriptions of perceived image character.

They are NOT diagnoses of a particular hardware technology.


============================================================
72. THE FINAL FOUR-PART MODEL
============================================================

Think of a display as:

    LIGHT
       +
    TIME
       +
    SPACE
       +
    PERCEPTION


LIGHT:

    spectrum
    brightness
    white luminance
    contrast


TIME:

    PWM
    DC dimming
    FRC
    temporal dithering
    pixel response
    motion


SPACE:

    pixel structure
    subpixels
    gamma
    black level
    white level
    local contrast
    surface
    AG sparkle
    reflections
    scattering


PERCEPTION:

    edges
    motion
    focus
    tracking
    attention
    blinking
    visual workload
    perceived stability


All four combine into:

    THE IMAGE YOU ACTUALLY SEE.


============================================================
73. THE MOST USEFUL MENTAL MODEL
============================================================

Do not ask:

    “Is IPS bad?”

Ask:

    “What does this IPS panel actually do?”

Do not ask:

    “Is FRC bad?”

Ask:

    “Is this display using temporal modulation, and can I
     observe it?”

Do not ask:

    “Is PWM bad?”

Ask:

    “Does this display's brightness modulation matter under
     my actual viewing conditions?”

Do not ask:

    “Is high contrast bad?”

Ask:

    “Is the luminance relationship clean and comfortable
     for this particular image?”

Do not ask:

    “Is low blue light enough?”

Ask:

    “What are the brightness, spectrum, temporal behavior,
     surface, and tonal behavior together?”


============================================================
74. THE CORRECT WAY TO EVALUATE A DISPLAY
============================================================

STEP 1

    Check brightness behavior.

STEP 2

    Check PWM.

STEP 3

    Check temporal stability / FRC.

STEP 4

    Check pixel response and motion.

STEP 5

    Check black level.

STEP 6

    Check white luminance.

STEP 7

    Check gamma and tonal transitions.

STEP 8

    Check local contrast and edges.

STEP 9

    Check surface texture and AG sparkle.

STEP 10

    Check pixel/subpixel rendering.

STEP 11

    Check spectrum / color temperature.

STEP 12

    Look at real text and real images.

STEP 13

    Compare the complete visual character.

STEP 14

    Ask:

        DOES IT LOOK SOLID, CLEAN, STABLE,
        CONTROLLED AND NATURAL?


If yes, that is the visual quality commonly described as:

    INK-LIKE.

============================================================
76. THE FINAL RULE
============================================================

Do not judge a display from one specification.

Do not judge it from one technology label.

Do not judge it from:

    IPS
    VA
    TN
    OLED
    8-bit
    8-bit + FRC
    “flicker-free”
    “low blue light”
    high refresh rate
    1 ms response
    high contrast
    high PWM frequency

Each specification describes only part of the system.

Instead:

    IDENTIFY THE PHYSICAL BEHAVIOR
          ↓
    SEPARATE TEMPORAL FROM SPATIAL BEHAVIOR
          ↓
    CHECK OPTICAL INTENSITY
          ↓
    TEST ONE VARIABLE AT A TIME
          ↓
    MEASURE WHEN POSSIBLE
          ↓
    LOOK AT THE ACTUAL IMAGE
          ↓
    COMPARE THE COMPLETE VISUAL RESULT


The ultimate practical question is not:

    “Does this monitor have the right specification?”

It is:

    “WHAT DOES THE LIGHT ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE?”

And then:

    “DOES THE IMAGE LOOK STABLE, SOLID, CLEAN,
     CONTROLLED, NATURAL, AND INK-LIKE?”


That is the point where all the individual engineering
variables come together.

PWM, FRC, dithering, pixel response, brightness, contrast,
gamma, spectrum, pixel structure, and surface coating are not
separate final answers.

They are different parts of the path that produces the final
image seen by the eye.
