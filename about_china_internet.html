What is going on in China?

- Access to popular global websites including android apps are blocked.

- International outbound bandwidth are strangled intentionally and it's more noticeable at peak hour (around 7.30 pm to 10.30 pm).

- Because international bandwidth is finite, traffic for foreign websites often gets the lowest priority compared to domestic traffic (like WeChat, Tencent, or Alibaba).

- Using corporate lines like IPLC/IEPL are needed to maintain stability and speed at peak hour.

- Blocking is stricter on Tier 1 city like Shanghai / Beijing / Guangzhou / Shenzen.

- Political Issues
	
	The "Sensitive Period" Sweeps: During major political events in China (such as the National People's Congress, anniversaries, or periods of social unrest), the GFW heavily ramps up its automated filtering.
	
	Blanket Bans: During these weeks, the firewall switches to an aggressive "block first, ask questions later" mode.
	
	Targeted Providers: Popular, cheap VPS providers used by developers (like DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, or Bandwagon Host) face massive, automated IP sweeps. Millions of IPs are blocked overnight, regardless of whether you were doing anything wrong.

- Budget sellers utilize several infrastructure strategies to make expensive routes look incredibly affordable:

	Massive Over-Subscription (Crowding): The provider buys a single, modest 50 Mbps IPLC or CN2 GIA bandwidth pipe and crams 1,000 budget users onto it. If only 20 people are using it at 3:00 PM, it feels blindingly fast. If everyone hops on at 9:00 PM to play games or watch videos, the provider forcefully throttles everyone's speed to keep the connection alive.
	
	NAT/Shared Port Systems: Instead of giving you an individual server or a dedicated IP, they sell you a specific port range on a shared machine. Hundreds of users share a single premium IP address, drastically cutting down the provider's IP hosting costs.
	
	Hybrid "Fake" Routing (Sling-Shotting): This is the most common trick. The seller routes your initial connection request over a premium IPLC or CN2 GIA line to quickly clear the Great Firewall. However, once the connection clears the border checkpoint, they immediately offload your heavy data downloads onto standard, cheap public BGP routes. You get a fast initial response time (low ping), but your overall download speeds are highly vulnerable to network congestion.
	
- If you purchase an ultra-cheap premium line proxy, expect the following trade-offs:

	The "Peak Hour" Slump: The service may perform flawlessly during the day, but will lag or drop connections from 8:00 PM to 11:00 PM when the shared pool gets overcrowded.
	
	Poor IP Reputation: Because hundreds of budget users share the exact same outbound IP addresses, those IPs get flagged quickly. You will constantly run into Google Captchas, Cloudflare verification screens, or sudden IP bans on platforms like Steam, PlayStation Network, or Netflix.
	
	Sudden Disappearances: Budget providers operate on razor-thin margins. If their upstream IPLC/CN2 provider raises prices or cuts them off for data abuse, the seller will often just shut down operations overnight without issuing refunds.

CHINA CARRIER NETWORKS & CROSS-BORDER ROUTING

1. THE BIG THREE OPERATORS (CARRIERS)

* China Telecom (CT)  : The largest landline/broadband operator. Owns the most
  massive international gateway capacity but suffers from
  severe public routing congestion.

* China Unicom (CU)   : Strong infrastructure, particularly in Northern China.
  Its standard international routes are often slightly
  cleaner and less congested than CT.

* China Mobile (CM)   : The largest mobile operator. Its international branch
  (CMI) has rapidly expanded fiber capacity, offering
  good routing to APAC hubs (e.g., Hong Kong).


2. THE TWO TIER HIGHWAY SYSTEM: NORMAL VS. PREMIUM ROUTES

* NORMAL ROUTES (Public Internet / Civilian Backbones)

- Characteristics : Massive bandwidth capacity, highly affordable, but shared
  by millions of standard residential users.

- Peak Performance: Terribly congested during China peak hours (18:00 - 24:00
  GMT+8). Packet loss can spike to 10%–30%; high jitter.

- Use Case        : Static files, large downloads, non-time-sensitive data.


* PREMIUM ROUTES (Enterprise / VIP Backbones)

- Characteristics : Isolated, dedicated express lanes with strict Quality of
  Service (QoS) guarantees. Completely distinct ASNs.

- Peak Performance: Stays ultra-stable 24/7. Latency remains flat and packet
  loss is near 0%, even during peak evening congestion.

- Use Case        : Live streaming, gaming, financial apps, corporate VPNs.


3. PREMIUM CATEGORIZATION (THE EXPENSIVE HIGH-END ROUTES)

CATEGORY A: CHINA TELECOM (CT) PREMIUM LINES

* CT 163 Network (AS4134)   -> [NORMAL] Standard public route. High congestion.

* CN2 GT (AS4809)           -> [MID-TIER] "Global Transit". Uses CN2 lines for
  the international hop, but dumps traffic back onto
  the congested AS4134 network inside China.

* CN2 GIA (AS4809)          -> [ULTRA-PREMIUM] "Global Internet Access". The
  gold standard. Pure AS4809 path both inside
  China and overseas. Absolute lowest latency.


CATEGORY B: CHINA UNICOM (CU) PREMIUM LINES

* CU 169 Network (AS4837)   -> [NORMAL] Standard public route. Decent, but
  congests at peak hours.

* 9929 Network / CNI        -> [ULTRA-PREMIUM] Enterprise-grade backbone.
  (AS9929 / A99)
  Features very low user load and exceptional
  stability, rivaling CN2 GIA for performance.

* AS10099 (CUG / CUP)       -> [GLOBAL AGGREGATION] China Unicom Global line.
  Used to collect international traffic and aggregate
  it at overseas hubs before entering AS9929/AS4837.


CATEGORY C: CHINA MOBILE (CM) PREMIUM LINES

* CMI Standard (AS58453)    -> [NORMAL] Good speed to nearby Asian hubs via
  public peering, but subject to unpredictable drops.

* CMIN2 Premium (AS58807)   -> [ULTRA-PREMIUM] China Mobile's answer to CN2 GIA.
  A newly built, highly isolated premium network
  specifically engineered to bypass public congestion.


4. CRITICAL PITFALLS & THE ULTIMATE LEVEL ABOVE BGP

* Asymmetric Routing Trap   : Watch out for "One-Way Premium" providers. They
  route traffic via expensive premium lines for the
  outbound return to China, but use cheap, congested
  standard routes for the inbound path. True premium
  requires bidirectional "Two-Way" (双程) routing.

THE ARTIFICIAL CHOKEPOINT: UNDERSTANDING CHINA-TO-HONG KONG NETWORK ROUTING

If you test a connection from Mainland China to Hong Kong, you will quickly notice
a geographic paradox: 
- Pinging the United States (across the Pacific Ocean): ~150ms (Normal/Expected)
- Pinging certain Hong Kong servers (literally next door): 250ms+ (Absurd)

This guide breaks down the hidden mechanics, artificial bottlenecks, and 
commercial gatekeeping that explain why cross-border networking between China 
and Hong Kong feels intentionally broken.


1. THE CORE PARADOX: THE CALIFORNIA DETOUR

When you ping an unoptimized Hong Kong IP from a city like Jiangsu, your packets 
do not take a straight path south across the Shenzhen border. Instead, they 
frequently embark on a massive international detour:

[Jiangsu] -> [Shanghai/Beijing Gateway] -> (Pacific Ocean) -> [Los Angeles/San Jose] 
                                                                    |
[Hong Kong] <- (Pacific Ocean) <- [Los Angeles/San Jose] <----------+

This physical journey across the Pacific Ocean and back is exactly why your latency 
spikes to 250ms. The Chinese carriers are deliberately forcing local traffic 
into public international transit loops.


2. THE MONOPOLY & THE "CHINA DIRECT" TAX

The three major state-owned carriers—China Telecom, China Unicom, and China Mobile—
control all physical fiber lines crossing the Mainland-HK border. They treat 
cross-border bandwidth as a premium luxury commodity rather than public infrastructure.

To manage this, they divide their networks into two distinct tiers:

A. The Public Dump (163 Network / AS4134 & AS4837)
   - This is the standard, cheap public broadband path used by regular everyday users.
   - The border pipes for this network are intentionally left congested, unoptimized, 
     or completely closed to certain HK data centers.
   - If a Hong Kong website or server (like speedtest.hk) relies on standard public 
     routing, their China-bound traffic is thrown into the 250ms "California Detour."

B. The Premium Highway (CN2 GIA / CMI / CU Premium)
   - These are dedicated, high-priority, border-optimized fiber routes.
   - Latency over these lines is flawless (30ms - 50ms from central China to HK).
   - *The Catch:* The carriers charge hosting companies and data centers an 
     exorbitant "China Direct" premium fee—often 10x to 20x the cost of standard 
     bandwidth—to access these clean lines.


3. WHY THE U.S. ROUTES BETTER THAN HONG KONG

It seems dishonest that a packet can travel to America faster than it can cross 
the street into Hong Kong. This happens because of how traffic is categorized:

- US Traffic = Bulk Commodity: China has massive, open-peering, high-capacity 
  undersea cables built directly to the US west coast. Standard public traffic is 
  allowed to use these freely because the carriers don't monetize standard US 
  routes the way they monetize the scarcity of Hong Kong access.
- Hong Kong Traffic = Premium Gatekeeping: Because Hong Kong is the primary 
  gateway for Chinese businesses looking outwards (and vice versa), the carriers 
  know they can demand extreme prices for direct border access. They artificially 
  constrict public routing to force businesses into buying expensive enterprise VIP lines.


4. THE GREAT FIREWALL (GFW) BOTTLENECK

Every single packet traveling between Mainland China and Hong Kong must pass through 
Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) at one of the massive international gateway hubs 
(Guangzhou, Shanghai, or Beijing). 

Because Hong Kong operates on an open, uncensored internet, the inspection 
bottlenecks at the HK border are significantly harsher and more congested than 
the bulk pipes leading out to the rest of the world. Standard public traffic sits 
in a massive digital waiting line at the border, while paid Premium (CN2) traffic 
gets fast-tracked through dedicated hardware.


SUMMARY FOR NETWORK TESTERS

- Does 250ms represent reality? 
  Yes, for small, independent websites or services that cannot afford the "China Tax."
- Does 250ms represent major apps/games? 
  No. Multi-billion dollar platforms (Tencent, Alibaba, NetEase) buy the premium 
  direct lines so their users experience a smooth 30-50ms connection.

The network performance you see between China and Hong Kong is rarely dictated 
by physics or geography—it is dictated entirely by commercial toll booths.

UNDERSTANDING VPS ROUTING TO MAINLAND CHINA: A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR NON-NETWORK ENGINEERS

CHAPTER 1 - THE BIGGEST MISCONCEPTION

Many people believe:
"My VPS is located in Singapore.
Therefore packets travel directly from Singapore to China."

Unfortunately, this is usually FALSE.

The Internet does not work by choosing the geographically shortest path.

Instead, every network chooses where to send your packets.

The decision is made by the network operator using BGP (Border Gateway Protocol).

Think of it like shipping a package.

A package from Jakarta to Nanjing may travel:

Jakarta → Singapore → Hong Kong → Nanjing

instead of

Jakarta → Nanjing

because the courier company has its own logistics network.

The Internet behaves the same way.


CHAPTER 2 - WHAT IS A VPS?

A VPS (Virtual Private Server) is simply a computer inside a datacenter.

For example:

Your Laptop → Internet → Singapore VPS → Internet → Computer in China

The VPS itself does not determine how packets travel.

The NETWORK connected to the VPS determines that.


CHAPTER 3 - DATACENTER ≠ NETWORK

This is one of the most important concepts.

Example:

Your VPS says:

Location:
Singapore

Datacenter:
Equinix

Many people assume:

Singapore = Excellent China route

This is NOT guaranteed.

Equinix is only the building.

Inside Equinix there are hundreds of companies.

Imagine Equinix as a shopping mall.

Inside the mall:

Google
AWS
Tencent
Alibaba
Cloudflare
NTT
PCCW
Many VPS companies

They all rent space.

Simply being inside Equinix says NOTHING about which network your VPS uses.


CHAPTER 4 - WHO ACTUALLY MOVES THE PACKETS?

Packets usually travel through several companies.

Example:

Your VPS → Provider Network → NTT → China Unicom → China Computer

NTT is called an INTERNATIONAL TRANSIT PROVIDER.

They carry traffic between countries.

Other major international carriers include:

NTT
PCCW
Arelion (formerly Telia)
Tata
GTT
Lumen
Cogent
Hurricane Electric

These companies own submarine cables, backbone routers and international links.


CHAPTER 5 - WHY DOES NTT APPEAR SO OFTEN?

NTT is enormous.

They have major Points of Presence (PoPs) in:

Singapore
Hong Kong
Tokyo
Osaka
Taipei
Seoul

Many VPS providers simply buy bandwidth from NTT.

This is completely normal.

There is nothing "wrong" with using NTT.

It is simply a GENERAL PURPOSE international backbone.


CHAPTER 6 - WHAT IS A "MIDDLEMAN"?

Suppose your traffic is:

Singapore VPS → NTT → China Unicom → Nanjing

NTT is acting as the international carrier.

Many people call this a "middleman".

This is completely normal.

Nearly every budget VPS has at least one transit provider.


CHAPTER 7 - DOES HONG KONG REMOVE THE MIDDLEMAN?

No.

This is another common misunderstanding.

A Hong Kong VPS may still be:

Hong Kong VPS → NTT → China Unicom

or

Hong Kong VPS → PCCW → China Telecom

or

Hong Kong VPS → Arelion → China Mobile

The physical location does not determine the routing.


CHAPTER 8 - WHY ARE CHINA ROUTES EXPENSIVE?
Chinese carriers have international companies.

China Unicom → China Unicom Global (CUG)
China Telecom → China Telecom Global (CTG)
China Mobile → China Mobile International (CMI)

These companies sell premium international connectivity.

Their customers are usually:

Cloud providers
Banks
Enterprises
CDNs
Large VPS providers

NOT ordinary consumers.

Your VPS provider buys bandwidth from them.

Then resells it to you.


CHAPTER 9 - WHAT DOES "CHINA OPTIMIZED" MEAN?

A China optimized VPS usually has better connectivity into mainland China.

Instead of

Singapore → NTT → China

it might be

Singapore → China Unicom Global → China Unicom → Nanjing

or

Singapore → China Telecom Global → China Telecom → Nanjing

This usually reduces:

Latency, Packet loss, Jitter

Especially during busy hours.


CHAPTER 10 - DOES PREMIUM ALWAYS MEAN FASTER?
NO.

This is extremely important.

Premium routing only means:

The provider purchased a better path.

They can STILL oversell it.

Imagine this ...

Provider buys:

10 Gbps premium China bandwidth.

Then sells:

1000 VPS

Each advertised as

100 Mbps.

In theory:

1000 × 100 Mbps = 100 Gbps

But the provider only owns:

10 Gbps.

During peak hours:

Everyone shares the same link.

Performance becomes poor.


CHAPTER 11 - WHY DO THINGS BECOME SLOW AT NIGHT?

The usual peak hours are approximately:

7 PM → 2 AM China time.

This happens because millions of users begin using:

Streaming
Gaming
Cloud services
Video
Downloads

The international links become congested.


CHAPTER 12 - WHY PING IS NOT EVERYTHING

Many beginners only look at:

Average Ping

Example:

100 ms

looks good.

But this tells only part of the story.

Much more important:

Packet loss
Jitter
Latency consistency

Example A:

100

Very smooth.

Example B:

But the connection feels much worse.


CHAPTER 13 - WHAT IS JITTER?

Jitter means:

How much the latency changes.

Low jitter:

100

100

101

High jitter:

95

103

Remote desktop becomes laggy.

Voice calls stutter.

Games feel inconsistent.


CHAPTER 14 - WHAT IS PACKET LOSS?

Packet loss means:

Some packets never arrive.

Even:

1%

can be noticeable.

3%

becomes annoying.

5%

usually feels bad.

Packet loss is often worse than adding 20 ms of latency.


CHAPTER 15 - UNDERSTANDING TRACEROUTE

Traceroute shows:

Which routers your packets visit.

Example:

Singapore → NTT Singapore → NTT Hong Kong → China Unicom → Nanjing

This is completely normal.


CHAPTER 16 - WHY DO SOME HOPS SHOW 80% LOSS?

Example:

Hop 5

80%

Hop 6

90%

Destination

0%

This DOES NOT mean your connection has 80% packet loss.

Many routers simply ignore traceroute replies.

Always look at:

THE FINAL DESTINATION.


CHAPTER 18 - CAN VPN FIX A BAD ROUTE?

Sometimes.

Usually not.

VPNs cannot reduce congestion.

They only change WHICH path is used.

Example:

Without VPN:

Singapore → NTT → China

With VPN:

Singapore → China Unicom Global → China

Now performance may improve.

The VPN itself is not the reason.

The underlying network is.


CHAPTER 19 - WHAT ABOUT "AIRPORT" SUBSCRIPTIONS?

Many Chinese "Airport" providers advertise:

CN2

CMI

CUG

CTG

These providers usually purchase premium enterprise connectivity.

Your traffic then uses THEIR network.

Sometimes this is much better.

Sometimes it is only marketing.

Always test.

Not immediately.

For most people:

Step 1

Buy a normal Singapore VPS.

Test it.

If it performs well,

stop.

Only upgrade if you actually experience problems.

Network quality.

Priority #2

Routing into China.

Priority #3

Packet loss.

Priority #4

Jitter.

Priority #5

Latency.

CPU and RAM are MUCH less important.

For a relay server:

CHAPTER 24 - THE SIMPLEST MENTAL MODEL

Think of Internet routing as airline travel.

Your destination is Nanjing.

The VPS provider chooses the airline.

Some airlines fly:

Singapore → Hong Kong → Nanjing

Others fly:

Singapore → Tokyo → Shanghai → Nanjing

Others have direct partnerships.

The airplane (WireGuard, VPN, Hysteria) does NOT choose the route.

The airline (network provider) does.


FINAL TAKEAWAYS

1. A VPS location does NOT determine routing.

2. Equinix is a datacenter, not an Internet provider.

3. Most inexpensive VPS providers buy transit from companies like NTT or PCCW.

4. Seeing NTT in a traceroute is completely normal.

5. Hong Kong VPSs can also use transit providers.

7. Premium routes can still perform poorly if the provider oversells them.

8. Packet loss and jitter matter more than a small difference in average ping.

9. VPNs do not magically improve the Internet. They only help if they use a better underlying network.

10. The best approach is to test a standard VPS first. Upgrade to a premium China route only if real-world performance during peak hours is unacceptable.
